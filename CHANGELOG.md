# Wildlore: These Isles, release notes

Newest first. The README is the front page; this is the long memory.

## What's new in 5.0: Every Door

- **Trumps.** Mum finds your gran's old cards (a creature on each, five numbers underneath), and around half the homes, pubs and inns of the isles will play you after a natter. Pick a stat, higher number wins, and the winner pockets both cards. Beat someone and their card is yours, plus a few quid once a day.
- **The album.** A Trumps cards page in the Almanac lays out every region's set, silhouettes for the ones you're missing, and a prize (a coat or a chapbook) for finishing a region.

## What's new in 4.6: Dressed for the Weather

Your own character, clothes of these isles to wear, creatures whose sex you can actually see, and seven more things
to play at the places they really happen.

- **Who's setting off?** A new game now asks who you are: male or female, build, skin tone, hair style and colour,
  and the colour of your top, with a Randomise button and a live preview. Old saves keep the look they had.
- **The Wardrobe.** Change your hair in the mirror at Mum's, and dress from what you own in the Wardrobe (pause
  menu). Shops show you trying things on before you buy. What you wear shows in the world, on the VS card and on
  your journeys, and letters now get your title right.
- **Boutiques.** Fourteen clothes shops round the Isles stocking what the place is known for: Harris Tweed in
  Stornoway, Fair Isle in Lerwick, Aran jumpers in Galway, Welsh wool, Donegal tweed caps, a Derry shirt, Northampton
  brogues, kilts in public tartans, wellies in colours, a sou'wester for Whitby weather. Shopkeepers and a few
  passers-by notice what you've got on.
- **Male and female creatures.** The summary page says it plainly, the sign sits beside every creature's name (Team,
  battle, Ark, Fieldbook), and pairing tells you straight out that it takes one male and one female, and why a pick
  won't do. Forty-two species now look different by sex, as the real animals do, with a line about it in the
  Fieldbook and a tick for seeing both.
- **Crown green bowls** at the Waterloo in Blackpool and greens in the Potteries, Sheffield and Macclesfield, with the
  crown and the bias doing what they do. **Stone skimming** at Oban (for Easdale, home of the World Championships) and
  on Llyn Padarn.
- **The seaside arcade** on Blackpool's Golden Mile and Brighton's Palace Pier: a 2p pusher, a claw with honest
  weakness and penny falls, change from the counter, a daily limit, and soft toys to win. It is also honest about
  what the machines keep.
- **The village fete:** cheese rolling down a Cooper's Hill, bog snorkelling (Llanwrtyd Wells' idea, 1985) and welly
  wanging, any day for a small prize and bigger on the real date.
- **Curling** at Inverness rink, taught by doing (the house, the button, the hog line, sweeping), and **Cumberland
  and Westmorland wrestling** at Grasmere Sports: read the hipe, the buttock and the cross-buttock, best of three
  falls, embroidered drawers and all.
- The approved Welsh lessons (Sut mae, Pontcysyllte, Trefor) and the remaining wave 45 facts are in, and the Nightly
  fuzz no longer sets you down on a lease fence.

## What's new in 4.5: Learn as You Go

The big one. Wildlore has grown from "a Pokémon game set in Britain" into a creature-battling RPG of these isles that
quietly teaches you things while you play. None of it is homework: it's in the people you meet, the places you go and
the creatures you catch.

- **Smoother creatures and a lovelier world.** Every creature is redrawn at twice the detail with soft edges and
  shading. The world gets light that moves through the day, cloud shadows, shore foam, swaying trees, firelit rooms and
  a lantern in caves (all off on Low, which looks as before).
- **A little Welsh, Gaelic, Irish and Scots.** Teachers in their own countries offer short, optional lessons, a quick
  "Say it" after some battles, a word of the day and certificates: Welsh in Wales, Scottish Gaelic in the Highlands and
  Islands, Irish in the Gaeltacht, Scots in the Lowlands, Ulster Scots in Antrim, and a taste of Manx, Cornish,
  Jèrriais and Guernésiais. Every Fieldbook entry now gives the real animal's name in Welsh, Irish and Scottish Gaelic,
  and a true "In real life" fact.
- **More creatures.** 33 new evolutions (some only in a season, a place, or once you hold a language certificate), and
  crossbreeds generated when you pair two related creatures, following real biology: close relatives cross, most
  crossbreeds can't have young, and Dot explains why.
- **Biology you can practise.** Dr Keir's challenges ask you to predict the odds before an egg hatches; a Biology page
  covers DNA, mutation, inbreeding and hybrid vigour; and a little moor shows natural selection at work.
- **People of these isles.** A "These Isles" page and 37 new plaques, people and quests on the peoples who made these
  islands: Celts, Romans, Norse, Huguenots, Jewish communities, Travellers, the Famine and emigration, Liverpool's
  Chinese seamen, Tiger Bay, Windrush and more.
- **How these isles work.** Fifteen plain-words topics on the UK versus Great Britain, devolution, Ireland, the Good
  Friday Agreement, the open border, Brexit, the Crown Dependencies and the Overseas Territories, told even-handedly by
  the people who live with them, and a cake to deliver from Derry to Donegal.
- **Isles of Invention.** A Timeline of Invention from Boyle to AlphaFold that hands you working gadgets, science-fair
  puzzles (binary, a cipher, DNA), Bletchley Park, and a quest on what AI is and isn't.
- **Money, explained.** An Economist's notebook fills in as you trade: supply and demand, compound interest and APR,
  the winner's curse at auctions, and the casino's real odds on every table.
- **Things worth knowing.** Short, kind lessons from people you meet: spotting a scam text, reading a payslip,
  budgeting, renting versus buying, gambling harms, a bad week, grief and asking for help (with real helpline numbers).
- **Arts and letters, land and sky.** Poets' Corner (Dylan Thomas, Burns, Yeats, Heaney, the Brontës and more), and the
  geology, weather and climate of these isles, with a forecasting challenge and the Shipping Forecast.
- **Kin abroad and neighbours.** Fly to the Welsh of Patagonia, Gaelic Cape Breton, Irish Boston and Ulster-Scots
  Appalachia, or take the ferry to Brittany. Walk from Gibraltar into La Línea, and visit Cyprus: the British bases,
  Pyla in the buffer zone, and both sides of Nicosia, each with local money, time and words.
- **50 facts corrected** across the game's plaques after a full accuracy audit.

Saves from 4.4 carry straight over.

- **Tidier settings.** The easier-minigame options and Reduce flashing now sit under Accessibility, alongside text size and the readable font.

## What's new in 4.2: Far and Wide

- **Local time abroad.** Fly out to the Overseas Territories and the sky follows their clock, not Britain's: it can be night in the Falklands while it's afternoon in Cheshire, and Bermuda keeps American summer time. The departures board tells you what time you'll land, the pause menu shows local time while you're away, and a big jump in hours brings on jet lag (a nap sorts it out).
- **Regional forms.** Some creatures look and behave a little differently depending on where you catch them: Irish hares and stoats, Shetland, Hebridean and North Ronaldsay sheep, Guernsey, Highland and Kerry cattle, a Manx cat with no tail, Skomer's own vole, Moray Firth dolphins, Bermuda's skinks, and folklore cousins like the Welsh pwca, the Manx cabbyl-ushtey and the Orkney selkie. They're logged in the Fieldbook as forms, so they don't change your count of 200.

## What's new in 4.1: Voices of the Isles

- **The local language, where it's spoken.** Welsh across Wales, Irish in the Republic (the Gaeltacht first), Scottish Gaelic in the Highlands and Islands, Manx on Mann, Cornish in Cornwall, Jèrriais and Guernésiais in the Channel Islands: town names on the arrival banner, signs on the buildings that matter, greetings from the locals, all with English alongside. - **Accessibility.** Larger text sizes, the Atkinson Hyperlegible font for easier reading, and an easier mode for every timing minigame (fishing, darts, bells, shanties, the caber...).
- **Share it.** Save your postcards and photos as pictures with captions, and try the weekly challenge: a fixed seed and a set of rules, with a result code to compare with everyone else.

## What's new in 3.6: Good Stock

- **Breeding, done properly.** Every creature now carries its makings: genes for coat, tint, markings, a hidden knack, size and build, two copies of each, one from each parent. Pair two fond creatures at Guiting Rare Breeds Farm in the Cotswolds, walk the basket off, and see what hatches.
- **It's real genetics.** Dominant and recessive, carriers, co-dominant roans, incomplete dominance, sex-linked tortoiseshells (XY in beasts, ZW in birds, as in life), linked genes, the odd mutation, hybrid vigour, and what happens if you breed too close. A Punnett square shows the odds before you pair, and the Stud Book fills in as you see the results for yourself.
- **Hybrids, where they'd really happen.** The wildcat and the moggy (a real problem in Scotland), red and sika deer, mountain and brown hares, mules and hinnies (sterile, as they are), a wholphin, and a couple from folklore. They get their own section of the Fieldbook and sprites drawn from both parents.
- **The Roslin Institute** near Edinburgh, home of Dolly the sheep, where Dr Keir explains the clever bits; conservation quests for rare breeds; a Breed Class at the County Shows, stud fees, a genotype test, and a paddock you can buy.

Your creatures already have their makings, worked out from who they are, so old saves just carry on.

## What's new in 3.5: Open for Business

- **Buy a business.** A chippy in Whitby or Blackpool, a B&B in Keswick or Portree, a fishing boat out of Whitby or Dingle, a market stall in Bath or Carlisle, or an allotment in York or Lancaster. Keep it stocked (your own catch, your own veg, what you buy at the market), upgrade it, and empty the till when you pass. Coach parties, storms and gluts happen.
- **And pay for it.** Business rates come weekly, statements monthly, and once a year a brown envelope from HMRC that explains self assessment better than HMRC does.
- **Twelve Regulars** who remember you: a retired trawlerman, a Welsh choir mistress, a Belfast shipyard pensioner, a Dublin bookie and more. Win them round and they write, turn up in other towns, and one day offer to mind one of your businesses for you.
- **Darts** in the pubs, with a league in each nation, and **crabbing** off harbour walls with a summer competition.
- **The allotment show and sheepdog trials** at the County Shows.

Saves from 3.4 carry straight over.

## What's new in 3.4: Market Day

- **Prices depend on where you are.** Things are cheap where they're made and dear a long way off, so a pannier of Bakewell puddings bought in the Peak fetches more in Cardiff. Sell too much in one place and you'll flood the market; fresh things go off. The radio's Money Box tells you who's paying what, and there's a market report on Mondays.
- **25 real market halls**, from Leeds Kirkgate and Cardiff Central to the Barras, Belfast's St George's and the English Market in Cork, each with a stall-holder who has opinions.
- **The Building Society.** Borrow at honest compound interest (it explains APR in plain words), save a bit, and get a strongly worded letter if you fall behind. Mum will lend you a little once, interest free, guilt included.
- **Trades.** You're getting better at things: Rambler, Trader, Cook, Angler, Naturalist and Local, each read off what you already do, with small perks along the way. See the Trades page on Stats.
- **Fishing.** Get a rod, cast at the water and land one of 37 real fish, from brown trout to mackerel (and some only found overseas). A few creatures only turn up on a line. There's an easier-fishing option in Settings.
- **Report a bug** from the pause menu, a Linux build alongside Mac and Windows, and a CONTRIBUTING guide for anyone who fancies helping.

Saves from 3.3 carry straight over.

## What's new in 3.3: Overseas, and a new name

The game is now **Wildlore: These Isles**, because that's what it's about. Your saves don't notice.

- **RAF Brize Norton.** Win the Moot and an RAF movements officer at Brize Norton, south of the Cotswolds, signs you up for the departures board.
- **Fly to the Overseas Territories**: Gibraltar and its macaques, the Sovereign Base Areas on Cyprus, Ascension, St Helena (say hello to Jonathan), Tristan da Cunha, the Falklands, South Georgia, a research station in the British Antarctic Territory, Bermuda, the Cayman Islands, Turks and Caicos, the British Virgin Islands, Anguilla, Montserrat, Pitcairn and Diego Garcia. Each has its own landscape, wildlife, plaques, trainers and a quest, and the territories have new creatures of their own.
- **Jersey, Guernsey and Sark** by ferry from Poole Quay, open once you've won four Stones.
- **Real money for every one of them.** US dollars on the BVI, Turks and Caicos and Diego Garcia, Bermudian and Cayman dollars pegged to the US dollar, Eastern Caribbean dollars on Anguilla and Montserrat, New Zealand dollars on Pitcairn, euros on the Sovereign Bases. Gibraltar, the Falklands, St Helena, Jersey and Guernsey print their own pounds at par: they'll take your sterling, pay you in theirs, and the mainland won't touch it. There's a bureau in every territory and at Brize Norton.
- The right flags on the right flagpoles, and no puffins in Bermuda.

- Today's Walk on the wireless: a creature that's out in numbers, a pub with a doubled quiz prize and a weather bounty, new every real day. No streaks, nothing lost if you miss one. Rematches and quiz prizes now reset by the real date
- Proper signs on the buildings: painted shop fascias, pub signs on brackets, enamel station boards, carved stone over the museums, and the name of the place pops up when you're at its door
- A gentler climb in the middle: more trainers before the two big jumps in level, and the Team Thermos now shares three-quarters of the XP
- **2.0, Common Ground.** The Enclosure spent the whole game fencing the land off. Now you get stuck in:
  - Rewilding: six damaged places, one in each nation (a drained moss in the Peak, a clear-fell in Snowdonia, an overgrazed glen, a choked river, a cutaway bog and a scraped bank on the Isle of Man). Help the warden and the place changes in front of you, stage by stage, until the creatures that left come back
  - A parish noticeboard in every town, with daily jobs (count the bats after dark, find some blackberries, no questions asked). Jobs earn standing with England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, Ireland and the Isle of Man, and standing gets you a discount, extra stock, a parcel from the landlord, and the locals knowing your name
  - Battles with more to think about: call the weather yourself (Call the Clouds, Proper Downpour, The Haar, Beast from the East, Bank Holiday Sun), drain, recoil, flurries, Batten Down, and a knack for every creature line, like Thick Fleece, Stiff Upper Lip and Moaner
  - Uncommon coats: about one wild creature in a hundred turns up in a seasonal coat, winter white, autumn russet, summer gold, spring green, storm dark or moonlit silver
  - Home: a trophy shelf at Mum's that fills up as you go, a garden out front to grow your forage in, and the legends you've caught dropping by
  - County Shows: a different one each real day (the Cheshire, the Royal Welsh, the Great Yorkshire, the Royal Highland, Balmoral and the Ploughing), with a marquee, a ring, a rule of the day and rosettes to spend. After the credits, the Open Moot at Stonehenge, five fights straight, as often as you like
  - Post from Kit: sixteen postcards waiting at post boxes as the story goes on, and every Keeper (and a few Enclosure Directors, who are trying their best) has a little side story once you've moved on
  - Every menu fits its frame now, even if you call your creature something long
- **2.1, First Impressions.** The depth was there; now you can see it:
  - A cold open on a moonlit moor, Mum and Garside pointing you at the noticeboard and the garden, a first job you can do in the Forest, and an "Around here" line on the pause card for jobs, shows, rewilding and side stories
  - Keepers fight like bosses: their own movesets, a held charm, a trick that suits the place, and a cup of tea for the ace. Maud's spa is dressed for a swimming gala
  - Battles you can read: the weather plate says what the weather does, moves say when they're stronger, stat changes and crits show up, snares give you honest odds, and the wobbles are real
  - Moves look and sound like what they are (beams, bolts, clouds, drains, a sky that changes when you call the weather), twelve new attack sounds, and longer tunes for the first hour
  - The wild changes with the real seasons: spring fledglings, summer swifts and glow-worms, autumn starlings over Chat Moss, winter snow buntings, and adders and bats that sleep through winter. The Rumours page tells you when
  - Each starter has a knack and a signature move of its own, evolved creatures are properly stronger, and wild creatures sometimes leave a bit of forage behind
  - The Cat and Fiddle moor has gritstone edges and heather now, and the Field Station has the owl and a kettle
- **2.2, Fine Detail.** Your own creature from behind is a proper creature now, not a brown blob: tails, stripes, flames and markings on all 200 back views. The map is a real map of the Isles (nations, uplands, rail and ferry lines, and little markers for shows, rewilding sites and waiting post). Every Keeper's venue is dressed as a real place, from a rugby terrace in Cardiff to a candlelit crypt under York Minster, with a crowd that changes its tune once you've won. The shop opens on the shelves, Mum's trophy shelf shows off your rosettes, and the heather has calmed down a bit
- Options for the rest of us: every key and button can be remapped, type symbols as well as colours, a colour-blind palette, instant text, and a setting to calm the flashing down
